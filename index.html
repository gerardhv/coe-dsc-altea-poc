Centre of Excellence for Data Sharing & Cloud (CoE-DSC)

Who we are

One central hub in the Netherlands to find answers to data sharing and cloud challenges

The Centre of Excellence for Data Sharing & Cloud (CoE-DSC) supports organisations with establishing trust mechanisms for secure and trusted data sharing and usage of cloud, ensuring that they can tap into the business opportunities this transformative practice offers. Our mission is to unlock the true value of data sharing by supporting organisations in realising scalable data spaces, the next evolutionary concept in data sharing. Through the development of generic building blocks and collaborative efforts, we enable easier and cost-effective implementation of data spaces across sectors and geographies.

Support creating economic and social value

The CoE-DSC is the central hub in the Netherlands to find answers to data sharing and cloud challenges. We bring together the expertise and achievements of parties like the IDSA hub, the data sharing working group AIC4NL and the Gaia-X Hub Netherlands. By uniting forces, we aim to prevent fragmentation and provide the Dutch data sharing community with a centralised hub of knowledge, tools, use cases and expertise. In this way we support the developments of sovereign data sharing to create both economic and social value.

Trust mechanisms

Why they matter

Sharing data between organisations — especially sensitive data between parties that may not know each other — only works when all participants are confident that data is used according to agreed conditions and that misuse is prevented. Trust mechanisms are what turn that confidence from an assumption into an enforceable, verifiable reality. They are foundational to data sovereignty: without them, data sharing and the data economy simply won't take off (Bernadette Farias Lóscio, CoE-DSC/TNO).

What trust mechanisms include

  • Identity & attestation management — ensuring participants are who they say they are
  • Definition and enforcement of usage and access policies — data used only under pre-defined conditions
  • Legally binding data sharing agreements and contracts
  • Transparency, tracing and auditing — monitoring adherence to agreed standards
  • Accreditation of trust service providers
  • Onboarding and certification of participants

Together, these form the backbone of a trustworthy data infrastructure in which participants know what to expect and can share data with confidence that it will not be misused. Trust operates at multiple layers: peer-to-peer between participants, in the underlying technology, and in the overarching governance rules and their enforcement.
